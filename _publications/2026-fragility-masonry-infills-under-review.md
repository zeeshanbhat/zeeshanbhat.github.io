Masonry infills are highly vulnerable to collapse in out-of-plane (OOP) direction during earthquakes, posing significant economic losses and life safety risks. During a seismic event, these infills are subjected to in-plane (IP) damage in addition to the OOP inertial forces. The IP damage further increases their vulnerability to collapse in the OOP direction. This study investigates the seismic fragility of three types of masonry infills [burnt clay brick, fly ash brick, and autoclaved aerated concrete (AAC) block] in the OOP direction, considering the IP damage. The infills were considered to be located at different stories in reinforced concrete (RC) frame buildings, which were subjected to varying intensity levels of ground shaking.  Multiple Stripe Analysis of three-dimensional nonlinear models of 5-story and 8-story buildings with the considered types of masonry infills was carried out under bidirectional ground motions of different intensity levels to record floor accelerations and inter-story drift. Equivalent single degree of freedom (SDOF) systems representing the behaviour of masonry infills in the OOP direction were subjected to the recorded floor accelerations, taking into account the IP damage due to the corresponding inter-story drift. Median seismic collapse capacity was evaluated using fragility curves developed for all three types of masonry infills at different stories. The results indicated that infills at lower stories were more susceptible to collapse compared to those at upper stories, despite an increase in floor acceleration with height. This highlights that the OOP collapse of masonry infills is primarily driven by IP damage rather than by OOP inertial demand. Burnt clay brick and fly ash brick masonry infills showed similar collapse capacity, whereas AAC block infills demonstrated higher capacity due to their lower mass and floor accelerations.
