I developed seismic fragility curves of masonry infills in low- and mid-rise reinforced concrete (RC) buildings while explicitly considering the interaction between **in-plane (IP) damage** and **out-of-plane (OOP) collapse mechanisms** during earthquakes.

### Key Contributions

- Developed **seismic fragility curves incorporating coupled IP–OOP interaction**, addressing limitations of conventional approaches that treat these mechanisms independently.

- Performed **Multiple Stripe Analysis (MSA)** of **3D nonlinear RC frame models (5-story and 8-story buildings)** subjected to **bidirectional ground motions**.

- Extracted **floor accelerations and inter-story drift demands** from nonlinear analyses to capture the simultaneous effects of **OOP inertial demand and IP damage** on masonry infills.

- Implemented **Equivalent Single Degree of Freedom (SDOF) models** to simulate the **OOP response of infills while accounting for IP damage due to inter-story drift**.

- Developed **collapse fragility curves** for three common masonry infill types:
  - Burnt clay brick masonry  
  - Fly ash brick masonry  
  - Autoclaved aerated concrete (AAC) block masonry  

- Investigated the **influence of story location and building height** on the OOP collapse probability of masonry infills.

- Demonstrated that **IP damage is the dominant driver of OOP collapse**, with **lower-story infills exhibiting higher vulnerability despite increasing floor accelerations at upper stories**.
